Tool 1 of 5

Inventory Cost Identifier

Before calculating EOQ, build conceptual clarity. Inventory decisions involve three cost categories: ordering costs (incurred each time an order is placed), carrying costs (incurred to hold inventory), and stockout costs (incurred when inventory runs out). Classify each scenario.

Tool 2 of 5

EOQ Calculator

The economic order quantity minimizes total inventory costs by balancing ordering costs (which decrease as order size increases) against carrying costs (which increase as order size increases). The optimal order quantity is where these two costs are equal.

Tool 3 of 5

Safety Stock and Reorder Point Calculator

The reorder point tells you when to place an order. Safety stock is extra inventory held as a buffer against demand spikes or supplier delays. More safety stock reduces stockout risk but increases carrying costs.

JIT vs Traditional Inventory Analyzer

Just-in-Time inventory systems aim to eliminate waste by receiving materials only when needed. This reduces carrying costs but requires reliable suppliers and flexible production. Enter your inventory parameters to compare the financial impact of JIT versus traditional approaches.

Backflush Costing Engine

Backflush costing delays recording of costs until a trigger point -- either completion of goods or point of sale. This simplifies accounting in JIT environments where WIP inventory is minimal. Compare three trigger point approaches side by side.
