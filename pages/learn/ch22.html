Capital Budgeting and Cost Analysis

Evaluate long-term investment decisions using NPV, IRR, payback, and accrual rate of return; identify relevant cash flows; analyze the impact of taxes; and run sensitivity analysis on key project assumptions.

Learning Objectives

Tool 1 of 5

Relevant Cash Flow Identifier

Before running NPV calculations, build conceptual clarity. Only future cash flows that differ between alternatives are relevant. Sunk costs are always irrelevant. Allocated overhead is rarely relevant. Opportunity costs and working capital changes are usually relevant. Classify each scenario.

Tool 2 of 5

NPV and IRR Calculator

Net present value discounts all future cash flows to today and subtracts the initial investment. A positive NPV means the project earns more than the required rate of return. IRR is the discount rate that makes NPV equal zero -- accept the project if IRR exceeds the required rate of return.

Tool 3 of 5

Payback Period and AARR Calculator

Payback period is the time required to recover the initial investment. Accrual accounting rate of return is average annual operating income divided by investment. Both are simpler than NPV but have serious limitations -- payback ignores cash flows beyond the payback date and AARR ignores time value of money entirely.
