Pricing Chapter 14

Pricing Decisions and Cost Management

Evaluate short-run and long-run pricing decisions, compute cost-plus prices, set target costs from market-based target prices, and manage product costs across the full life cycle.

Not started

Learning Objectives

  1. Identify the major influences on pricing decisions
  2. Distinguish short-run pricing decisions from long-run pricing decisions
  3. Calculate cost-plus prices using alternative cost bases and markup formulas
  4. Apply target pricing, target costing, and value engineering to reduce cost before production
  5. Use life-cycle costing to understand profitability across the full value chain
Tool 1 of 4

Pricing Context Identifier

Pricing decisions depend on context. In the short run, managers focus on incremental revenues and incremental costs. In the long run, prices must cover all value-chain costs and earn a target return. Classify each scenario.

Cost-Plus Pricing Calculator

Cost-plus pricing begins with a chosen cost base and adds a markup. In long-run pricing, the markup should recover costs excluded from the base and provide the required return on invested capital.

Target Costing and Value Engineering

Target costing starts with the market price customers are willing to pay, subtracts target operating income, and produces the allowable target cost. Value engineering then searches for design and process changes to close the cost gap.

Life-Cycle Profitability Planner

Product costs are often locked in before production begins, while revenues and support costs occur over several years. Life-cycle costing evaluates profitability over the full value chain rather than a single accounting period.
